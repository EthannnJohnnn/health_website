What is CPR?

Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) is an emergency procedure performed when a person's breathing or heartbeat has stopped. CPR helps maintain vital blood flow to the brain and other organs until professional medical help arrives.

Basic CPR Steps

Responsiveness

Tap the person's shoulders firmly and ask loudly, "Are you okay?" Look for any reaction, movement, or normal breathing.

Call for Help

Call local emergency services immediately, or explicitly point to someone nearby and command them to make the call.

Check Breathing

Tilt the head back slightly. Look, listen, and feel for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds before acting.

Compressions

Place both hands in the center of the chest. Push hard and fast (100–120 compressions per minute). Allow the chest to fully recoil.

Important Reminder: CPR should ideally be performed by trained individuals. Seek formal CPR training to learn the correct, life-saving techniques.
